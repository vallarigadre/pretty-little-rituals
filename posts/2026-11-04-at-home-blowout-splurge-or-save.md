## The technique, step by step

The tool matters, but the method decides whether you get a blowout or a puff. Here is a simple order that works with either budget.

1. **Rough dry first.** Dry your hair with a dryer or the brush on a lower setting until it is about 70 to 80% dry. Styling soaking-wet hair takes longer and puts more heat on each section.
2. **Section it.** Clip your hair into four parts: two at the back and one at each side. Work in sections about as wide as your brush.
3. **Tension and direction.** Place the brush under each section at the root, then pull slowly toward the ends while the air flows down the hair shaft. Pointing the air down, from root to tip, is the easy secret to glass hair, because it smooths the cuticle instead of roughing it up.
4. **Cool shot.** Finish each section with a few seconds of cool air while it is still wrapped around the brush. This helps the shape set.
5. **Polish.** Once your hair is fully dry, smooth a drop of serum or oil through the lengths for shine, avoiding the roots.

**How long does it take?** On shoulder-length hair, plan on about 20 to 30 minutes from damp to done. Longer or thicker hair can take 35 to 45 minutes, especially while you learn. A blow-dry brush tends to be quicker than a dryer and round brush, because you dry and style in one pass. Most people get faster after a few weeks.

## FAQ

### Is a blow-dry brush better than a hair dryer and round brush?

Neither is better, they suit different people. A blow-dry brush is easier to learn and quicker because it dries and styles at once. A dryer with a thermal round brush takes more practice but gives you more control over shine and shape.

### What is the difference between a thermal brush and a regular round brush?

A thermal brush has a metal or ceramic barrel that warms up under the dryer's airflow, so it helps set the shape as you roll. A regular round brush usually has a solid or plastic core and relies on the dryer alone.

### How do I get "glass hair" at home?

Start with heat protectant on damp hair, work in small sections, keep tension on the brush, and point the airflow from root to tip. Finish with a cool shot and a drop of serum on the lengths. Smooth technique matters more than the price of the tool.

### Do I need heat protectant with a hot air brush?

Yes. Any tool that uses heated air on your hair is still heat styling, so a protectant belongs in the routine whether you use the Airwrap, the Revlon or a dryer and brush.

### How can I make my blowout last longer?

Sleep on a silk or satin pillowcase, tie your hair in a loose high bun at night, and use dry shampoo at the roots on day two. Avoid steamy showers without a shower cap, since moisture is the fastest way to lose a smooth finish.
