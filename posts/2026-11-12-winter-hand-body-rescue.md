## Step 3: Smooth rough spots

Elbows, knees, heels and the backs of the arms tend to go rough and bumpy in winter. A gentle exfoliant a few times a week helps lotion sink in and leaves skin feeling smoother. In winter, less is more: skip harsh scrubbing, and never exfoliate skin that is cracked or irritated.

## Build your kit

If you only want a few things, here is how I'd prioritize:

1. **A hand cream by every sink.** This alone makes the biggest difference to how winter feels.
2. **A body lotion applied right after the shower.** Timing matters more than the price.
3. **A gentle exfoliant two or three times a week** for rough elbows, knees and arms.
4. **A creamier wash or shower oil** if your skin feels tight after every shower.

At night, a thick layer of hand cream under a pair of soft cotton gloves is an old-fashioned trick that still feels wonderful.

## FAQ

### When should I put on body lotion in winter?

Right after a shower or bath, within a few minutes, while skin is still slightly damp. Pat dry with a towel rather than rubbing, then apply your lotion or cream straight away.

### Is hand cream or hand balm better for very dry hands?

Thicker textures tend to feel more protective in winter. Use a richer cream or balm at night and after washing up, and a lighter, faster-absorbing cream during the day when you need to type or text.

### How often should I exfoliate my body in winter?

Two or three times a week is plenty for most people. Skip any area that is cracked, sore or irritated, and always follow with a moisturizer.

### Can I use face moisturizer on my body?

You can, but body products come in bigger sizes and are usually more practical. A large pump lotion like CeraVe is easier to use generously on arms and legs than a small face cream.

### What helps cracked hands overnight?

Many people like a thick layer of hand cream or a rich balm under soft cotton gloves at bedtime. If cracks bleed, hurt or don't improve, see a dermatologist.
